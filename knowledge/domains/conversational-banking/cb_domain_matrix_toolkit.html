Can Conversational Banking be sold standalone? Yes — here is the honest boundary.

It does not require the Digital Banking platform or our domain services. The conversational layer consumes whatever the bank already runs — the proof is a decade of deployments on entirely non-Backbase stacks (J.P. Morgan, TD, Standard Chartered, DBS all ran the same conversational core we now own). What it needs from the bank is what the bank's own mobile app already has: content, identity, and APIs.

No dependency (L1)

D0–D3: content export, SSO/session, the read & write APIs behind the existing app, step-up auth. Nothing new is built — we plug into what serves the app today.

One data dependency (L2)

Spending-intelligence use cases need categorised transactions — the bank's existing PFM/enrichment source or a provider. We consume it; we don't require ours. Named prerequisite, never a blocker.

Optional platform expansion (L3)

End-to-end resolution needs some workflow/case capability, and growth plays need some decisioning — the bank's own (we integrate) or ours (Customer Operations / Relationship Intelligence). That is the expand conversation, never the entry ticket.

Price the call log, not the feature list.

A use case earns its place in the business case by appearing in your contact-intent distribution with volume and unit cost — not by demoing well. Customers still phone about things the app already does: industry-wide, only 14% of self-service attempts fully resolve, and even "very simple" issues resolve just 36% of the time (Gartner, 2024). The assistant's first job is capturing that failed demand.

The five value pools

Every use case is priced on exactly one primary pool — mixing pools is how business cases lose credibility.

V1

Deflection

A human contact avoided.

Counts: intents handled by phone/branch/chat today. Doesn't: tasks already self-served in-app.
V2

Completion

Failed self-service rescued.

Counts: proven drop-off. Doesn't: assumed delight.
V3

Revenue

Conversion, deepening, primacy.

Counts: measured uplift vs control.
V4

Risk & loss

Scams, fraud speed, disputes, arrears.

Counts: loss lines finance already tracks.
V5

Experience

CSAT / NPS / accessibility / 24×7.

Counts: strategically. Doesn't: as the headline.

The first three conversations

Where this tool earns its keep. Each conversation has a job, a set of questions, and one thing to leave with. Keep a KNOWN / ASSUMED / ASK register from day one — every number is one of the three, and the client always knows which.

CONVERSATION 1

Qualify & hook

Bring
  • The matrix (this tool) + two proof points matched to their market
  • The pair: only ~20% of calls automate without process rewiring — 25–40% reduction when properly integrated (McKinsey 2026)
Business questions
  • Is there a live cost-income or efficiency target this year? Who owns it?
  • What runs today — IVR, bot, neither? Bought when, renews when?
  • Gut-size it: how many calls a month, and what does a call cost you?
  • Which pain is loudest — cost, waiting times, complaints, scam losses?
Leave with: agreement to share a 2-week call-intent sample — "we'll show you what your calls cost, intent by intent."
CONVERSATION 2

Evidence & readiness

Bring
  • The data request (right column of every handout)
  • The dependency staircase — "here's how little we need to start"
Architecture questions (with their EA in the room)
  • Which read/write APIs already serve your mobile app? Are they internally consumable?
  • How does step-up auth work today — can a conversation reuse it?
  • Is there a transaction-categorisation source (PFM vendor, in-house, schemes)?
  • Where do disputes/cases live — which system would a conversation open a case in?
  • Any decisioning/eligibility APIs (pre-approval, offers)?
Leave with: the intent sample in hand + a D0–D5 readiness map of their estate.
CONVERSATION 3

The number & the wedge

Bring
  • THEIR intent-cost table (built from the sample)
  • The calculator, live, with their numbers
  • A 3+1+1 slate: three deflection anchors + one revenue play + one resolution loop
Economics questions
  • What threshold makes this "meaningful" for the budget owner?
  • Committed-plus-consumption pricing: comfortable paying for activity, not seats?
  • What would kill this internally — and who needs to see the number first?
  • Agree the falsifiers: "if the sample shows <X human contacts on addressable intents, the deflection case fails and we pivot to revenue/risk anchors."
Leave with: a named sponsor + a scoped 90-day proof (L1 slate) + success metrics agreed.

The engagement plays

Four repeatable motions — every handout names which one fits that use case.

Intent-Cost Sprint
1–2 weeks · data-led

Two-week call sample (or transcripts) → classify → the intent-cost table. The single artifact that is current-state, shortlist and baseline at once. Run it before any workshop if you can.

Value-Wedge Workshop
Half day · decision-led

Their call drivers overlaid on the matrix; pick the 3+1+1 slate; calculator live with their numbers; KNOWN/ASSUMED/ASK register out the door.

Architecture Readiness Whiteboard
2 hours · EA-led

D0–D5 checklist against their API estate. Output: dependency map, the 90-day scope, and the named prerequisite (if any — usually enrichment).

Commercial Sense-Check
1 week · deal-desk governed

Their volumes through the pricing shape → a budget band the sponsor can carry internally. Never invent price points; the proposal governs.

Why this is safe to put in front of customers

Guardrails on every turn

Financial-services-specific checks per conversational turn — no false promises, no tipping-off, vulnerable-customer handling built in.

Every action is authorised

Nothing executes without a policy check; actor, policy and outcome land in an immutable audit trail. Governance is an artifact, not a slogan.

Humans by design

Autonomy is tiered per use case. The assistant owns the routine; people own the exceptions — with full context handed over.

Deterministic where it matters

The biggest assistants run payments on deterministic rails with AI understanding the request — generative where it helps, governed where it counts.

The industry lesson baked in: in 2024 a major fintech's AI assistant absorbed the workload of ~700 agents in month one — then in 2025 publicly restored guaranteed human access after quality slipped on disputes and emotionally-loaded cases. The design above is that lesson applied in advance: automate the transaction, guarantee the human for the exception.
