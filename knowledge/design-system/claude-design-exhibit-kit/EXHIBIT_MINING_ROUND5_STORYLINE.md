# Exhibit mining — round 5: storyline and language (Sept 2026)

**Source:** the same BCG archive as round 4 (1,622 chronological screenshots; photo timestamps preserve slide order, so title sequences = complete deck spines, ~15 decks reconstructed). **Scope rule:** narrative CRAFT only. No BCG content, numbers, benchmarks, framework names (their proprietary methods stay theirs), client material, or verbatim sentences enter this repo. Beat grammar is expressed in fresh example titles written in OUR voice. Content intel verdict: expired vintage (2019-22) + wrong source under the evidence doctrine → not mined, ever.

## The one big lesson

**The title chain IS the deck.** In every strong archive deck, reading only the action titles in order produces a complete, sequenced argument — each title advances the claim, no title repeats or stalls. Codified as the **flip test** (now in the build skill): before building slides, write the title chain and read it aloud as a paragraph. If it doesn't argue, fix the chain, not the slides.

## Six storyline archetypes (beat grammar, ours to use)

Beats named generically; example titles are fresh, in our voice, one line each.

**A. The Ambition Walk** — transformation/renewal proposals. Beats: the moment of truth (why the old plan died) → the quantified ambition ("the 2029 book pays for the 2027 build") → the first milestone ("eighteen months is the first proof, not five years") → the value walk (waterfall: today → moves → target) → the pillars (one detail page each, serial template) → phases and gates → governance and team → why us, last and short. *Use for: renewals, Legacy→Banking OS plays, NBB/HBTF-class pursuits.*

**B. The Model-Bank Mirror** — benchmark-led case for change. Beats: expectations moved ("your customers rate you against their last best app") → the challengers' proof → the quantified hypothesis stated as a dare ("a bank this size can serve the base with a third of the effort") → the model bank (what best-in-class looks like, by the numbers) → the technology that gets you there → what it means for the operating model → backups carry the ledgers. *Use for: outside-in POVs, board pre-reads, Alden-style value evidence packs.*

**C. The Market Landscape Read** — market-entry / strategy studies. Beats: the executive rail (five dimensions, re-shown per chapter, light moving) → where the pools sit → which segments are worth it (attractiveness ledger with verdict glyphs) → who plays where (logo maps) → the whitespace → the entry options (2-3, one recommended band) → what we would do first. *Use for: profile-bank, market-context research, expansion POVs.*

**D. The Selection Funnel** — vendor/partner/option evaluation. Beats: criteria BEFORE contestants (weights fixed first — this is what makes the read defensible) → the long-list read (harvey matrix) → the shortlist profiles (serial template) → the deep scoring per criterion → the decisive factors (commercials last, explicitly) → recommendation, with the discarded named and the backups kept warm. *Use for: RFP displacement, build-vs-buy studies, co-existence reads.*

**E. The Serial Dossier Tour** — learn-from-the-field decks. Beats: the framing question → one dossier per player, SAME template, N times (kicker counts the series: "player 4 of 9") → the synthesis ("what all nine did in year one") → the implication for the client. Rhythm is the persuasion: by dossier five the room predicts the template and starts comparing on your terms. *Use for: competitive landscapes, CB vendor tours, reference walls.*

**F. The Workshop Arc** — facilitation decks. Beats: objectives (two, never five) → game rules (time-boxed, one card per exercise: objective band + exercise/hints/output columns) → the working exhibit (the room fills it) → restitution → prioritization on TWO axes only → what got decided, re-read aloud → next steps with owners. *Use for: ignite-cb sessions, Ignite workshops, SteerCo working sessions.*

## Title grammar (extends SKILL.md §5, same weight)

1. **Flip test** — the title chain read alone must argue the whole deck.
2. **Quantify the claim in the title when the claim is numeric.** "Monthly actives doubled, and first-time growth is slowing" beats "Strong user growth continues."
3. **Question titles only at pivots** — one per deck, maybe two ("What are we optimizing for?"). A question title admits the deck doesn't know yet; spend that admission where it buys tension.
4. **The dare-hypothesis** — early in outside-in decks, state the falsifiable ambition as a sentence the room can reject ("this bank can answer a million calls before they happen"). Pairs with the coral ILLUSTRATIVE tag until validated.
5. **Recap titles at chapter seams** — "Where we are: the read is done, pricing is next." The tracker rail (T36) carries it visually.
6. **Serial kickers** — "landscape · player profile 3 of 9"; the count is a promise of discipline.
7. **Claim in the title, consequence in the band.** Two-part titles wrap; our one-line law stands. The takeaway band is where the "so that" lives.

## Strapline/band beats per archetype

Bands land at: the ambition slide (A), the hypothesis slide (B), the recommendation slide (C/D), the synthesis slide (E), the decision slide (F). Everywhere else, prefer the so-what rail (T17) or silence. One band sentence, one line — unchanged law.

## Question-bank shapes (extends the Ignite question bank — shapes only)

- **Per-segment 5-question card**: segmentation worth serving · strategic priorities · disruption and acquisition exposure · efficiency pools · what changed since the last crisis. One card per LOB, same shape.
- **Maturity self-locate**: 4 stages per dimension, client marks their own square in-room (workshop exercise, pairs with T38 read-back).
- **"What good looks like" checklist**: customer's-perspective features left, bank's-internal-capabilities right — the two-sided readiness read.

## Deliberately NOT imported (and why the archive itself proves it)

- **Their sentence-level language.** The archive's weakest slides are its wordiest ("unlock significant value", "holistic approach", stacked triads). Our §5b voice law (say it aloud, halve it, no triads, no abstractions) stays superior and untouched. Take the spine, keep our voice.
- **Benchmarks, sizings, KPI values** — expired vintage, unverifiable source, evidence doctrine forbids. (KPI-library SHAPES — theme → metric → plain-words definition → target-setting note — may inform the ROI questionnaire's structure; values never.)
- **Named proprietary frameworks** — no BCG method names, ever, in any asset.
- **Exec-summary walls of prose** — where the archive front-loads 200-word summaries, we keep the titled-chain + tracker rail instead.
