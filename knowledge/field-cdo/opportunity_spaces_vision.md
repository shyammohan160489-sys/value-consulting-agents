# Field CDO — Opportunity Spaces & Vision

## The Core Insight

Banks don't buy platforms from vendors. They buy confidence from peers.

A CTO at a $20B regional bank who is staring at a 15-year FIS implementation does not want a demo. They want to talk to someone who has been in their chair, made the same decision, navigated the same politics, and can tell them what they wish they had known. That conversation — the one that happens between peers, not between buyer and seller — is the most valuable asset in enterprise sales. And it is exactly what Backbase does not systematically create today.

The Field CDO role exists to create that asset. Not as a sales motion. As a practice.

---

## The Gap This Role Fills

Today, the journey from "a bank is interested" to "Backbase is engaged" looks like this:

```
Bank has a challenge
    → Sales team reaches out (product-led, vendor-positioned)
    → Bank evaluates alongside 3-4 competitors
    → If Backbase wins the evaluation, Value Consulting engages
    → Ignite Inspire / Assessment / Proposal
```

The Field CDO changes the entry point:

```
Bank has a challenge
    → Field CDO has a peer conversation (experience-led, outcome-positioned)
    → Bank sees Backbase as a trusted partner, not a vendor in an RFP
    → Backbase enters the conversation early, often before competitors
    → Ignite Inspire / Assessment is a collaborative next step, not a sales pitch
```

The difference is structural. In the first model, Backbase competes. In the second, Backbase is invited. The Field CDO is what makes the second model possible.

**Industry parallels:**
- McKinsey's Senior Advisors — former C-level executives who open doors and add credibility to engagement teams
- Deloitte's Industry Leaders — practitioners who lead with experience, not methodology
- Accenture's Client Account Leads — senior industry figures who own the strategic relationship

Backbase's version is sharper: the Field CDO is not a generalist executive. They are a digital banking technologist who has lived the exact transformation the prospect is considering.

## Five Market Opportunity Spaces

### 1. The Core Banking Modernization Wave

**The landscape**: Thousands of US banks and credit unions run on legacy core platforms — FIS (Profile, Horizon, IBS, Code Connect), Fiserv (DNA, Signature, Premier, Cleartouch), Jack Henry (Symitar, SilverLake, Core Director). Many of these implementations are 10-20 years old. The middleware layers are brittle. The digital channels are vendor-provided afterthoughts.

**The opportunity**: Most of these institutions know they need to modernize but are paralyzed by the risk and cost of core replacement. Backbase's "surround and extend" proposition — build a modern digital engagement layer on top of the existing core — is exactly the right message. But it needs to come from someone who has evaluated and rejected the "rip and replace" approach firsthand.

**What the Field CDO brings**: Credibility to say: "I was at [bank]. We looked at FIS Digital One, we looked at core replacement, and here's why we chose to decouple the digital layer. Here's what worked. Here's what we'd do differently." That story, told peer-to-peer, is more persuasive than any analyst report.

**Sizing**: There are approximately 4,500 banks and 5,000 credit unions in the US. Of those, roughly 500-800 institutions in the $5B-$100B asset range are actively evaluating digital platform strategies. This is Backbase's sweet spot.

### 2. The Digital Engagement Layer Play

**The landscape**: Even banks that are not ready for core modernization are desperate for better digital experiences. Their customers compare them to neobanks, fintechs, and big tech. The branch is shrinking. Mobile is everything. And most banks are stuck with the digital channels their core vendor provides — channels that are functional but not competitive.

**The opportunity**: Backbase as a standalone digital engagement platform, independent of core modernization conversations. Faster sales cycle, lower risk for the bank, clear ROI in customer acquisition and servicing cost reduction.

**What the Field CDO brings**: The ability to frame this not as "buy another platform" but as "here's how a CDO thinks about decoupling channels from core to move faster." Innovation pipeline thinking — how to pilot, prove, and scale without betting the entire technology stack.

### 3. Community and Regional Bank Uplift

**The landscape**: Institutions in the $5B-$50B asset range are systematically underserved by the large platform vendors. FIS and Fiserv focus their best innovation on their top-tier clients. Temenos and other international players struggle with US regulatory nuance. These banks have sophisticated needs but limited technology teams.

**The opportunity**: These institutions are the most receptive to a peer conversation. Their CDOs and CTOs often know each other through industry groups (ABA, ICBA, state banking associations). A recommendation from a peer at a similar institution carries enormous weight.

**What the Field CDO brings**: Direct peer credibility. If the Field CDO comes from a bank of similar size and complexity, the conversation starts with trust already established. The CDO can speak to the specific constraints of regional banking — limited IT headcount, regulatory burden, vendor dependency — in a way that resonates immediately.

### 4. Credit Union Digital Transformation

**The landscape**: The credit union market has its own technology ecosystem (Symitar/Jack Henry dominant, Corelation Keystone emerging, CUSO-driven innovation). Credit unions are mission-driven organizations where trust and peer recommendation matter even more than in commercial banking. NCUA regulatory nuance adds complexity.

**The opportunity**: Credit unions make technology decisions by consensus and peer validation. They attend the same conferences (CUESolutions, NACUSO, GAC), read the same publications (CU Times, Credit Union Magazine), and talk to each other constantly. One credible voice in this community can unlock an entire market segment.

**What the Field CDO brings**: If the CDO has credit union experience or relationships, the network effect is powerful. Even without direct CU experience, a CDO who can speak to the technology challenges of member-centric institutions (which share many characteristics with community banks) can bridge the gap.

### 5. Innovation and Fintech Partnership Advisory

**The landscape**: Every mid-to-large bank has an innovation function — some formal (innovation lab, partnerships team, venture arm), some informal (the CDO's side project). These teams evaluate 50-100 fintechs a year. They pilot 5-10. They take 1-2 to production. Most of this effort produces learning but not impact.

**The opportunity**: The Field CDO has lived this. They have managed an innovation pipeline, evaluated fintech partnerships, run proof-of-concepts, and made the hard decision about what graduates to production and what gets killed. This experience is a standalone value proposition.

**What the Field CDO brings**: Advisory credibility on innovation process, not just technology selection. How to structure a PoC. How to evaluate build vs. buy vs. partner. How to manage the politics of innovation inside a bank. And critically — how Backbase's platform can serve as the integration backbone for fintech partnerships (via APIs, Backbase Marketplace, and the engagement architecture).

**Standalone offering potential**: This could become an advisory service independent of Backbase platform sales. A Field CDO running "Innovation Pipeline Workshops" at banks creates value, builds relationships, and naturally surfaces Backbase opportunities — whether or not the bank buys the platform immediately.

## The Thought Leadership Funnel

The Field CDO's market impact follows a distinct funnel that differs from traditional sales:

```
Layer 1: BROADCAST (Scale)
│   Published articles, conference talks, LinkedIn presence, podcasts
│   Reach: 1,000+ banking executives per quarter
│   Goal: Brand recognition, intellectual credibility
│
├── Layer 2: CONVERSATION (Targeted)
│   Executive dinners, advisory calls, peer roundtables
│   Reach: 30-50 executives per quarter
│   Goal: Trust building, challenge identification
│
├── Layer 3: EXPERIENCE SHARING (Qualified)
│   One-on-one deep dives, "here's what I did at [bank]" sessions
│   Reach: 8-10 executives per quarter
│   Goal: Specific challenge mapping to Backbase value
│
└── Layer 4: ENGAGEMENT (Converted)
    Ignite Inspire workshops, discovery sessions, assessments
    Reach: 2-3 per quarter (steady state)
    Goal: Qualified pipeline handoff to Value Consulting
```

**Critical design principle**: Each layer is valuable on its own. Even if a conversation never converts to pipeline, the brand credibility and market intelligence it generates has value. The funnel is not a pipeline — it is an influence model with commercial upside.

## Competitive Positioning Through the Field CDO

The Field CDO changes how Backbase competes:

### vs. FIS Digital One
FIS positions D1 as the modern replacement for legacy FIS cores. The pitch is: stay with FIS, just upgrade. The Field CDO counter: "I was on FIS. The Digital One migration is a core replacement disguised as a modernization. Here's why decoupling your digital layer gives you options that a single-vendor stack never will." This is a conversation only a peer can credibly have.

### vs. Temenos
Temenos leads with analyst positioning (Gartner, Forrester) and European banking references. The Field CDO counter: "Analyst rankings measure vendor capability, not your outcome. Let me tell you what actually matters when you're migrating a US bank with US regulatory requirements, US core integrations, and US customer expectations." Temenos has limited US practitioner credibility. Backbase + Field CDO fills that gap.

### vs. Fiserv
Fiserv is infrastructure. They own the plumbing (core, payments, network). Their digital offering is an extension of infrastructure. The Field CDO counter: "Fiserv will always optimize for their infrastructure revenue. Your digital experience should be independent of your plumbing decisions. Here's how to think about that separation."

### vs. Consultancies (Deloitte, Accenture, McKinsey)
Big consultancies advise. They don't build. The Field CDO counter: "We're not here to write a 200-page report. We're here to show you what's possible, prove the value, and build it. I've sat through enough consulting engagements to know the difference between advice and outcomes."

## Beyond Pipeline: The Strategic Value of the Role

The Field CDO creates value that does not show up in pipeline metrics:

### Market Intelligence
Every executive conversation is a data point. What are banks worrying about? Which vendors are winning evaluations and why? What regulatory changes are driving technology decisions? This intelligence — aggregated and shared with Product, Marketing, and Strategy — shapes Backbase's roadmap and positioning.

### Reference Network
As the Field CDO builds relationships, successful Backbase clients become referenceable. A CDO who can say "talk to the CTO at EverBank" or "here's what happened at [bank X]" creates a reference network that compounds over time.

### Talent Attraction
A credible Field CDO practice attracts other senior banking executives who want to make a similar career move. The role becomes self-reinforcing: success attracts talent, talent expands reach, reach generates pipeline.

### Product Influence
A Field CDO who talks to 50+ banking executives per quarter has an unmatched perspective on what the market needs. This feedback loop — from market to product — is worth as much as the pipeline the role generates.

## Long-Term Vision: From Role to Practice

### Year 1: Prove the Model
One Field CDO in the US. Primary focus: EverBank SCP + thought leadership + lead generation. Success metric: the model produces qualified pipeline that would not have existed without it.

### Year 2: Scale in the US
3-4 Field CDOs covering different segments (regional banks, credit unions, large banks) and geographies (East Coast, Midwest, West Coast, South). Shared playbook, coordinated thought leadership calendar, executive roundtable program.

### Year 3+: Global Practice
Field CDO pilots in EMEA (open banking / PSD2 context), APAC (mobile-first / super-app dynamics), and LATAM (rapid digital adoption / neobank competition). Each region adapts the model to local market dynamics while sharing the global playbook.

### The End State
A global network of 8-12 Field CDOs who collectively:
- Maintain relationships with 500+ banking executives worldwide
- Produce a steady stream of thought leadership that positions Backbase as the industry authority on digital engagement
- Generate 30-40% of new logo pipeline through peer-driven conversations
- Serve as strategic client partners for Backbase's most important accounts
- Feed market intelligence that shapes product and strategy

This is not a sales team. It is a practice. And it is the single most defensible competitive advantage Backbase can build — because it is built on people, relationships, and experience that cannot be replicated by a competitor with a bigger marketing budget.
