# Moderator's Brief — Nordic FinTech Forum AI Roundtable

**Helsinki · May 2026 · Chatham House · 30 minutes**

You have **13 slides and 30 minutes**. The deck is designed to work *two ways*:

- **Mode A · Interactive (the ideal):** you frame the story (beats 1–3), then the room drives ~21 min of discussion via the three question prompts. Insight slides serve as your live synthesis at the end of each Q→I arc. You close with the meta-conclusion (beat 5).
- **Mode B · Backup (cold room):** you walk all 13 slides as a guided POV in ~11 minutes. The deck has its own narrative spine that holds without audience participation.

The deck is the same in both modes. What changes is who's talking and how long.

---

## Before you start

### Pre-flight checklist (30 min before)

1. **Open the deck** — `nordic_fintech_forum_opening_pov.html`. Press `F` for fullscreen, `→` to advance, **`P` for presenter mode (the speaker notes live here)**, `O` for the overview grid. Every slide's notes follow the same template: **WHY / SAY / PAUSE-DO / DROP-INS / NEXT**. The Q slides also have three drop-in sub-questions that each telegraph a column of the next insight slide — use them when discussion deflates.
2. **Notepad ready.** You'll capture quotes from the room live — these become the Helsinki Principles.

### Decide your mode in the first 60 seconds

After your cover slide, you'll know which mode you're in:
- **Engaged room → Mode A.** Let the three questions drive the room; insight slides become your live synthesis.
- **Tired room, end of day → Mode B.** Walk all 13 slides as a guided POV in ~11 minutes.

The deck doesn't care which mode you pick. Just commit.

## The story arc (memorise the shape)

```
  BEAT 1 — DISTURB        (slides 1–3)    Set up the trap. AI seems like a moat. It isn't. We've seen this movie.
  BEAT 2 — OBSERVE        (slide  4)      Positive pivot: you've built strong products. The architecture is what's shifting.
  BEAT 3 — REFRAME        (slide  5)      Here's the chain. Bottom-up history → top-down future.
  BEAT 4 — VALIDATE       (slides 6–11)   Three Q+Insight cycles, one per chain link.
  BEAT 5 — LAND           (slide  12)     The binary choice — bolt-on lose, re-platform win.
                          (slide  13)     Close — take-home line.
```

If you only remember one thing: **the chain is the spine.** Trust → Intent → Orchestration → Share of wallet. Every slide either sets it up, validates it, or lands it. Pull the discussion back to the chain whenever it drifts.

**Why the positive pivot (beat 2)?** After two slides of "you're in a trap," the room needs validation. Beat 2 says: your products are good — the problem isn't what you've built, it's what's missing between the things you've built. That earns the right to introduce the chain.

**The bottom-up/top-down framing on the chain slide is the most important sentence in the deck:** *Banks competed bottom-up — Wallet, then Mind. AI flips it: Trust first, and the chain compounds back to a bigger wallet.* Trust here = digitising the 1-1 RM relationship at scale. If the AI advises a customer, you must explain HOW it advised. That's what gave the RM authority — now it's a product feature.

## Talk track — slide by slide

### Beat 1 · DISTURB

#### 1 · Cover (≤ 30s)
> "Two ground rules: Chatham House — quotable, not attributable — and this room outnumbers me ten to one in talking time. Thirteen slides. We move fast. Let's go."

Don't introduce yourself. The badge does that.

#### 2 · The Trap (60s)
> "Three signals the AI feature race is already over. Under 5% performance gap between frontier models on banking-relevant tasks — the model is not the moat. Roughly 100% of global top-50 banks have shipped a GenAI assistant since 2024 — the feature is saturated. About 18 months until feature parity — your copilot will look like your competitor's. That's not a forecast; it's the roadmap horizon every analyst is publishing."

Move directly to slide 3. Don't pause for reaction yet.

#### 3 · The Pattern (75s)
> "Before anyone says 'this is different' — it isn't. AWS, 2008 onwards. EC2 reached parity with Azure and GCP within 24 months. The silicon became a line item; the orchestration layer became the largest infrastructure business in history. Same with Spotify. Every catalogue became identical. Spotify won on intent orchestration — Discover Weekly, Release Radar. 675 million users. Same pattern: the intelligence layer commoditises. The orchestration layer wins."

If anyone in the room has lived through AWS migrations, they will nod. That's your signal to move on. The trap is set.

---

### Beat 2 · OBSERVE (positive pivot)

#### 4 · The Architecture is shifting (90s — the slide the team really likes)

**Visual cue:** the three cards have **two voices** stacked. The top line is the bank-architecture spine (what the bank builds — telegraphs Backbase Banking OS). The italic line below is the customer moment (Barry's accessibility ask). Read both.

> "Here's our observation. The architecture of banking is shifting. You've built strong products — onboarding here, lending there, wealth elsewhere. Each product works. But the customer doesn't see your silos. They see one bank, and they're about to start asking it for advice.
>
> Three layers.
>
> PRODUCTS — strong, but isolated. Vertically built, in silos. The products work; the connections between them are manual. From where the customer sits: one bank, three apps, advice that's only ever a slice.
>
> TRUST — the connective tissue. Identity, consent, risk, compliance — not as checkboxes, but as shared infrastructure that connects everything. Reusable across products, markets and channels. And here's the test: when the AI advises a customer, the bank that can explain HOW — provenance, peers, history — earns trust. The one that can't, doesn't.
>
> INTENT — the emerging frontier. AI that doesn't just report or respond — it acts, within governed boundaries. Approves a top-up the moment the facility allows. Books a wealth review when a life event triggers. Trust converts to the next product sale.
>
> Value is migrating from product to trust to intent. The banks that connect trust to real-time decisioning at scale earn the customer's next move."

Walk the three cards left-to-right. Point at each label. After "earn the customer's next move" pivot to slide 5: *"And here's where the next decade is won."*

This is the slide that earns the room's attention. Don't rush.

---

### Beat 3 · REFRAME

#### 5 · The Chain (90s — bottom-up history flips to top-down future)
> "Banks competed bottom-up. You started at the floor — Share of Wallet. The mobile wave gave you Share of Mind. That's the layering most of you built. AI flips it. From now on, you build top-down — and the chain compounds back to a bigger wallet.
>
> Link one — TRUST. Stops being a brand claim and becomes plumbing. Here's the key: trust is how you **digitise what the RM did one-on-one**. When AI gives advice to a customer, you have to explain HOW it reached that — provenance, consent, oversight. That's what gave the RM their authority. Now you instrument it at scale.
>
> Link two — INTENT. Customers, and increasingly customers' agents, hand banks a mandate to act. Trust-backed intent converts higher than trust-free intent. That's the dependency.
>
> Link three — ORCHESTRATION. Hand-offs across customer, employee and agent without dropped context. The moat lives in the seams.
>
> Link four — SHARE OF WALLET. Better trust → better intent → better orchestration → bigger wallet → more data to improve trust. The loop closes. Three questions to test each link."

Walk the four cards left-to-right. End on "the loop closes" — that's the line that travels.

### Beat 4 · VALIDATE (3 × Q→I cycles)

For each cycle: **read the question, pause, then either let the room run (Mode A, ~6 min) or advance to the insight (Mode B, 5s pause).** When the room talks, capture quotes verbatim — these become the Helsinki Principles.

#### 6 · Q1 · Trust
> "Link one — trust. When the model is the same as your competitor's, what makes you the trusted counterparty?"

**Mode A probe (if silence):** "Name the capability — not the value, the capability."

#### 7 · I1 · Trust is the new infrastructure
**Mode A:**
> "What we hear in rooms like this: trust is moving from claim to KPI. THE GAP — one in three firms is at L3+ on AI governance. Two-thirds cannot prove how their AI decides. THE SHIFT — here's the framing that lands. Explainability, provenance, oversight — these are what gave the relationship manager their authority. Now they become product features that scale. **You're digitising what the RM did one-on-one.** Apple priced privacy as a product. Mastercard sells AI-graded trust as infrastructure. THE EDGE — and this is for the room. The Nordics built BankID and Swish — over 85% adult reach on shared identity and shared payment rails. You cooperated on the plumbing and competed on the experience. Trust-as-infrastructure here is an export, not a cost."

**Mode B:** same script — without pause for room reaction.

End on EDGE — let it land. Optional show-of-hands: "How many of your banks actively measure trust as a KPI — not just comply with GDPR?"

#### 8 · Q2 · Hand-off
> "Link three — orchestration. Where in your bank would the customer × employee × agent hand-off break — today?"

**Mode A probe:** "Be specific. Which system, which moment, which seam?"

#### 9 · I2 · The moat is in the seams
> "Banks digitised channels in the 2010s. What they didn't digitise was the context BETWEEN channels. That's where the orchestration moat lives. WHERE IT BREAKS — context dies between systems. Customers re-explain themselves to bots, call-centre agents, RMs, underwriters. Faster cars on the same broken roads. WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE — context travels with the customer. Every party — bot, banker, underwriter, the customer's own agent — opens the same view. The call becomes confirmation, not discovery. WHERE TO ATTACK — start at the seam where the next product or the next renewal is decided. Make context travel across THAT seam first. Compound from there."

If anyone said something useful in discussion, attribute back to them (seat number, not name). Land the seam frame — "the moat lives where channels meet, not within any single one."

#### 10 · Q3 · Agent's choice
> "Link four — share of wallet, through the agent's eyes. If an agent shopped this mortgage for your customer, would it pick you?"

**Mode A:** the question that makes the room twitch. Let the silence stretch. Probe both sides: "Or would your bank refuse to transact with an agent at all?"

#### 11 · I3 · When someone else owns the intent
> "When the bank doesn't own the intent layer, someone else does. Klarna — owned the checkout moment. The bank extending credit became invisible; Klarna became the brand on the screen. 150 million consumers. Plaid — the API between customers and their banks. One in four US adults has connected an account through it. Every fintech app routes through Plaid; banks became the backend. M-Pesa — Safaricom captured trust and intent before Kenyan banks modernised. It moves around 60% of Kenya's GDP. Banks remain — they are no longer the layer customers interact with. Look at your customer relationship today. Is there already a Klarna, a Plaid, a Tink, an M-Pesa circling it?"

Don't take answers — let the discomfort sit. Move on. The pattern is the point, not the example.

---

### Beat 5 · LAND

#### 12 · The Choice (90s — the single most important slide)
> "Across the banks we've seen — and we've watched this play out at thirty-plus institutions — this is binary. The banks that bolted AI onto product silos lost. Faster cars on the same broken roads. Channel-by-channel agents, five copies of the customer, AI fluent at the front, broken at the back. NPS plateaus. Cost-to-serve doesn't move. The banks that won re-platformed around the customer life. One identity, one consent, one customer record. An orchestration layer any AI agent — yours or someone else's — can plug into. Consistent CX from app to branch to advisor. Lift compounds — NPS, NTB, cost, ROE move together. This isn't a digital programme. It's an identity shift. **The decision is not whether to use AI. It is what AI plugs into.**"

After "what AI plugs into" — full stop. 5-second pause. Don't elaborate. Advance to the close.

#### 13 · Close (≤ 30s)
> "The bank that orchestrates trust and intent wins the agent's choice — and the customer's wallet."

**Mode A:**
> "Five things I heard you say tonight…" — read 3–5 quotes from your notepad. Their words, not yours. That's the Helsinki Principles. "Thank you."

**Mode B:**
> "Thank you. Find me at the bar."

## Facilitation moves (Mode A)

| When… | Move |
|---|---|
| Someone dominates | "Let me park that — really good — and pull in someone who hasn't spoken." |
| Vendor shopping | "Park the technology — what would have to be true *organisationally* for that to work?" |
| Too abstract | "Concretely — at your bank, what's the next decision a director has to make?" |
| Room agrees with you too quickly | "Let me argue the other side. If trust is just compliance dressed up, what changes?" |
| Backbase customer is in the room | Don't name them. Redirect to industry-wide questions. |
| Question doesn't land | Move to the insight slide. The narrative still flows. |
| Insight slide pre-empts the room | "We came with a view — but the better answer is in this room. What did we miss?" |
| Room is on fire and won't stop | Drop the choice slide. Let the room land its own conclusion. Read the close. |

## One last thing

The story arc is doing work even when the room can't. Beat 1 disturbs. Beat 2 reframes. Beat 3 demonstrates. Beat 4 validates. Beat 5 lands. That sequence carries a tired room as well as it carries a hot one — that's why the deck has 13 slides instead of 5.

The insights aren't conclusions — they're synthesis. Deliver them as "where rooms like this tend to land," not "here's the answer." That keeps the room honest, and keeps you honest.
